FL-005: The 33-Trade Setup (Three-Level Expansion → Reversal) What this setup isThe 33-Trade is a multi-day expansion and reversal pattern, commonly described as a “pump and dump.”It consists of:three consecutive rises or drops, without pullbacks in-betweenfollowed by aggressive exhaustion and reversalThe defining feature is structure repeating at multiple levels:three rises or drops, usually over three daysand three pushes within the last riseThis setup works to the upside or downside – everything below applies inversely. How the structure forms (the core logic) Rise 1 (or drop)Price forms a W (or M) baseA first rise occurs (after the W)Price then enters consolidation (not retracement)Rise 2A second rise continues in the same directionAgain, no pullback but instead consolidation followsRise 3A third, extended riseMomentum appears to accelerateInside Rise 3, you can identify three internal pushes (often visible on lower timeframes)These pushes will be high volume vector candlesThis is what gives the setup its name:3 rises × 3 pushes in the last rise = “33” What price behaviour should look like This setup does not stair-step with deep pullbacks.You are looking for:up → sidewaysup → sidewaysup (extended) → sidewaysThink staircase, not swings.Each rise:price closes closer to the highlittle to no meaningful retracementpressure continues to build What confirms exhaustion After the third rise:Usually a large wick to the top to trap tradersWe pull away from the wick but go into consolidation The consolidation lasts longer than usualYou begin to see wicks forming against the trendwicks to the top in an up-movewicks to the bottom in a down-moveMomentum stalls despite prior aggressionThis is your warning that distribution is underway. The trigger logic The trade is taken against the trend, at exhaustion.Typical trigger:an inverted hammer (or equivalent rejection candle)forming at Rise Level 3This is often:fastaggressiveunforgiving if you hesitateThis is not a setup where you wait for a perfect M or W on the reversal.You are making a judgment call based on exhaustion. Why entries feel uncomfortable Both sides of this move are aggressive:the final push up is fastthe reversal down is fastIf you wait for confirmation after the move starts, you are usually late.This is why the trade often has to be taken off the hammer. Targeting logic This setup uses EMA-based targets.Common sequence:First target: 50 EMASecond target: 200 EMAWhere possible:align EMA targets with Market Maker candles or obvious liquidity levelsexpect reaction at the 200 EMADo not assume price must break the 200.The move can end there and reset. What happens at the 200 EMA At the 200 EMA:reassessdecide whether to:exit fullyreduceflip biasor stand asideThis level often marks:the end of the dumpor the start of a new trendThere is no automatic continuation rule. Timeframe flexibility The 33-Trade can appear on any timeframe.Key rule:EMAs are always relevant to the timeframe you’re tradingExamples:15-minute setup → use 15-minute EMAs1-hour setup → use 1-hour EMAs5-minute setup → use 5-minute EMAsIf the structure isn’t obvious:change timeframedon’t force clarity What this setup is NOT not a single-session patternnot a quick scalpnot a clean M/W reversalnot something that appears oftenThis is a rare, high-impact structure, not a daily occurrence. Common mislabels Calling a two-day move a 33Ignoring the internal three pushes on Day 3Shorting too early (before exhaustion)Expecting a clean M/W on the reversalAssuming price must break the 200 EMA How this shows up in Discord When someone says:“This might be a 33”The correct questions are:Have there been three consecutive rises?Did each day end near the high?Is Day 3 showing internal three pushes?Are we seeing exhaustion wicks, not continuation?If not – it’s not a 33.
